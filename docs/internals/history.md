# History

Ritornel separates raw listening evidence from derived plays so play-counting rules, time-zone assignments, and identity fixes can always be recomputed without losing or mutating what happened. Pure derivation and play-rule logic live in `crates/ritornel-domain/src/history.rs`, and the stream matcher and its dry run in `crates/ritornel-domain/src/{streams,stream_report}.rs`; SQLite ingest, derivation, queries, scrobble delivery, timekeeping, and export live in `crates/ritornel-engine/src/{history_store,history_streams,history_heard,history_pages,history_stream,history_facts,import_store,stream_import,delivery_store,export,timekeeping}.rs`; server Last.fm workers, staged imports and RPC handlers live in `apps/server/src/{lastfm,imports,dispatch/history,dispatch/lastfm,dispatch/imports,dispatch/export}.rs`; CLI commands live in `apps/ritornelctl/src/{history,lastfm,export}.rs` and `apps/ritornelctl/src/spotify/`.

See [`docs/internals/schema.md`](schema.md) for the immutable history tables, [`docs/internals/playback.md`](playback.md) for listen staging and outbox delivery, [`docs/internals/identity.md`](identity.md) for scrobble matching, and [`docs/internals/plugins.md`](plugins.md) for the `ritornel-lastfm` plugin boundary.

## Observations and derivation

Listening history enters `library.db` through two seams: ingest (`Observations::{Listen, Imports}`, write method `history.ingest` in `history_store.rs`) for listens and Last.fm's scrobbles, and a run of Spotify's streams applied (`imports.apply`, "Spotify streams"). Each record is stored verbatim in `history_observation` (`payload_json`) plus its kind-specific detail table (`observation_listen`, `observation_import`, or `observation_correction`), keyed uniquely by `(endpoint_id, source_key)`. Re-ingesting the same key with an identical payload is a no-op; a conflicting payload under an existing key is recorded in `history_ingest_conflict` without altering the original observation.

Pure derivation (`ritornel_domain::history`, `GROUPER_VERSION = 5`) turns durable observations, the Spotify streams in force, active corrections, `listener_zone_period`s, and current `source_item` bindings into `play_event` and `play_event_observation` rows:

1. **Bind:** Each listen chain or import resolves its recording and optional track appearance through its `source_item`'s current `assignment` (following `recording.canonical_id`), unless overridden by an active `set_recording` correction. Unbound items still become `play_event` rows with `recording_id = NULL`.
2. **Chain:** Listens linked by `continues` join into one chain when the later listen starts within `CHAIN_WINDOW_MS` (24 hours) of the earlier listen's end. A move can continue a listen for the same listener on another endpoint. Cross-player dates allow the existing five-second clock tolerance. If the source shut down while waiting for release, its record keeps the handoff pause time as well as the shutdown time. A continuation on another player uses the pause. A restart on the same player uses the shutdown. When two endpoints reuse a key, only an endpoint-local match is authoritative. The chain takes its start time from the first (`primary`) listen and sums `ms_played` across all listens (`corroborating` for subsequent parts).
3. **Echo matching:** Imported scrobbles that match recorded deliveries from this library (`history_delivery` in `sending`, `accepted`, or `unknown` on the same endpoint, matching exact whole-second `sent_timestamp_s` and normalized artist + title via `same_track` / `words_key`) are paired 1-to-1 deterministically per `(endpoint, listen)` across matching delivered listens (`pair_echoes`, preferring active imports over excluded duplicates) and join each paired listen's play as a `corroborating` observation instead of creating a duplicate play. Any extra import beyond the matching delivered listens stands as a play of its own.
4. **Streams:** Each listen Spotify reported is one play, its repeats corroborating it, and a scrobble the stream matcher joins to it corroborates it too ("Spotify streams" below). The stream speaks for the play.
5. **Judge (`PlayRule`):** Listen chains are judged by `PlayRule::CURRENT` (`PlayRule::V2`, version `2`): a track counts when `track_duration_ms > 30_000` and (`ms_played >= track_duration_ms / 2` or `ms_played >= 240_000`). The outcome (`Outcome` in `ritornel-domain`, `PlayOutcome` in the contract) is `listened` (counted), `skipped` (did not count and `end_reason.moved_on()`), `partial` (did not count otherwise), or `unknown`. Imported scrobbles, and streams joined to one, count by `source_verdict`; a stream alone is judged by `streams::judge_alone`, which version 2 added.
6. **Exclude:** Any play containing an observation targeted by an active `exclude` correction is marked `excluded = 1`.

Every observation of a play that is not its primary records how it joined it, in `play_event_observation.joined_by`: `chain`, `echo`, `repeat`, or the matcher's tier, `start`, `during` or `renamed`, or `pin` for a correction's join.

Each `play_event.public_id` is a deterministic 16-byte hash derived from its primary observation's endpoint and `source_key`, so play IDs are deterministic for a given grouping, though a grouper or chaining change that alters the primary observation can change a play's ID.

## Time and time zones

Every derived play separates the UTC instant from the local calendar date and records the provenance of each (`clock_basis`, `zone_basis`, `calendar_basis`, `time_precision_ms`, `time_anchor`).

### Clocks and monotonic age dating

Every listen and every delivery attempt records three clocks (`crates/ritornel-engine/src/timekeeping.rs`):

- Wall clock (`started_at_utc_ms`, `sent_at_utc_ms`).
- Sleep-counting monotonic clock (`started_mono_ms`, `ended_mono_ms`, `sent_mono_ms`): `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` on Linux, `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` on macOS, and interrupt time on Windows (all continue counting through system sleep).
- Boot ID (`boot_id`, `sent_boot_id`): the machine's kernel boot ID, falling back to the server run ID where unavailable.

When dating a listen (`date_listen`), if the listen was delivered in the same boot it was heard in (`sent_boot == boot` and `sent_mono_ms >= started_mono_ms`), the hub dates it by age: `received_at_ms - (sent_mono_ms - started_mono_ms)` (`ClockBasis::HubAge`, `HUB_AGE_PRECISION_MS = 1_000` ms), making the result immune to a wrong sender wall clock. Otherwise it uses `started_at_ms`, subtracting `clock_skew_ms` when skew exceeds `SKEW_TOLERANCE_MS` (5,000 ms) (`ClockBasis::ServerWall`, `SERVER_WALL_PRECISION_MS = 5_000` ms). Imported scrobbles use their source UTC timestamp (`ClockBasis::SourceUtc`, `SOURCE_SECOND_PRECISION_MS = 1_000` ms).

### Local dates and zone periods

Local calendar fields (`local_date` as `YYYYMMDD`, `month_day` as `MMDD`, `local_hour`, `local_dow`, `utc_offset_minutes`) are computed using `jiff`'s bundled IANA time-zone database (never the host OS database) so derivation produces identical results on any machine:

- **Listens (`zone_of`):** Prefer a recognized non-UTC IANA zone captured on the listen (`ZoneBasis::Captured`). If the captured zone is missing, unknown, or UTC, look up the listener's most specific covering `listener_zone_period` (`assumed_zone`, `ZoneBasis::Assumed`). If no period matches, fall back to a captured UTC zone (`ZoneBasis::Captured`), or leave local date unset (`ZoneBasis::None`).
- **Imports (`import_time`):** Use `assumed_zone` from `listener_zone_period` (`ZoneBasis::Assumed`), or leave local date unset (`ZoneBasis::None`) if no period covers the timestamp.
- Clients manage periods via `history.zones` and `history.setZones` (which rederives plays in the same write).

## Incremental updates and rebuilds

- **Incremental:** Ingesting a listen derives only that listen's chain in the same transaction; identity resolution updates affected items (`rebind`); delivery state transitions rederive matching echo imports (`rederive_delivery`).
- **Full rebuild:** `history.rebuild` (also run automatically on server startup via `history_rule` in `apps/server/src/server.rs` whenever any play's `play_rule_version` or `grouper_version` differs from the build's current version) drops and rederives all `play_event` and `play_event_observation` rows in a single transaction. `history.rebuild` supports `dryRun: true` to report counts without writing.

## Queries

History views query `play_event` directly using its partial indexes (`play_event_recent`, `play_event_counted`, `play_event_on_this_day`) and the `play_effective` view, and the lists of what was heard read each play's links to it ("History by artist and album"); there is no rollup table to keep in sync:

| Query / Module | Behavior |
|---|---|
| `history.recent` (`history_store.rs`) | Cursor-paged recent plays (`PlayCursor`), newest first, including unbound plays and per-listen chain details. |
| `history.onThisDay` (`history_store.rs`) | Counted, non-excluded plays matching a `MMDD` `month_day` across all years. |
| `history.artists`, `history.albums` (`history_heard.rs`) | Every artist or album heard, with files or not: its key, its name, the library's ID when it is the library's, its counted plays, and its first and last local dates, by plays, first heard or last heard, paged (`HeardListParams`, 100 by default and at most 1,000). Read from the index on the links alone. A play stamped after now, by a wrong clock, counts and is never first or last, as on a page. |
| `history.artist`, `history.album` (`history_pages.rs`) | Taken by the library's ID, or by a name's key (`HistoryArtistParams`, `HistoryAlbumParams`): a key that names exactly one of the library's artists a name can be, or one of their records, answers with it, and otherwise with the plays heard under the name, with no records ("History by artist and album"). Banner summary (`Phrase`), yearly play counts (`YearPlays`), and top 6 artist records (`RECORDS = 6`). Counts only bound, counted, non-excluded plays, an artist's by the credit of the appearance a song was heard from, or else of its recording, as the lists link them; uses a 26-hour boundary window (`ZONES_APART_MS`) around the first and last instants so first/last local dates remain accurate across time zones. A play whose UTC instant is after now (stamped by a wrong clock) is counted as one with no instant: it is in no year and is never the first or last play, so the sentence never puts a future month in the past tense, and `firstPlayed` and `lastPlayed` are left out when no play is at or before now. |
| `history.track` (`history_facts.rs`) | Track and release history facts for the playing track (play counts, time-of-day patterns, and album history). As on pages, a play whose UTC instant is after now is counted as one with no instant: never the song's last time, the record's first, or its last before today. |
| `history.stream` (`history_stream.rs`) | Monthly play totals across the entire history (bound and unbound), layered by the year each artist was first heard, naming the top 3 artists per layer-month and excluding catch-up sync days with more than `SYNC_DAY` (300) plays. |

`ritornelctl history` prints recent plays directly, and exposes `artist`, `album`, `artists`, `albums`, `on-this-day`, `rebuild`, `zones`, and `zone {add, clear}`.

## History by artist and album

Every play is linked to the artists and the album it was heard as, whether or not there are files for them (`history_heard.rs`). The links are derived: written with the plays by `insert_plays` and dropped with them.

| A play's | Is linked to | And when the library has none |
|---|---|---|
| Artists | When it is bound to a song: each artist of the credit of the appearance it was heard from, or else of the recording, as canonical | Its names' artist, by its key |
| Album | When it was heard from an appearance: its release's record | Its names' album, by its key, under its artist's name. None when the play named no album |

- **Keys:** `artist_key` is the normaliser's key with accents folded and a leading "The" and a featured credit left out; `album_key` is the normaliser's key with accents folded (`normalize.rs`). They are narrower than the scrobble matcher's loose key, which also drops spaces and every "and": safe beside a title, and not on a name alone.
- **Rows:** `heard_artist` and `heard_album` hold a library artist or record by ID, or a name by its key and its spelling played most, counted plays first. A play links to each of its artists in `play_event_artist` and to its album, if any, in `play_event_album`, each link copying whether the play counts, its local date and its instant, so a list is read from the index on the links that begins with the listener (`play_event_artist_listed`, `play_event_album_listed`) without touching `play_event`, and a name's page from the one that begins with its row (`play_event_artist_heard`, `play_event_album_heard`). A row nothing links to is deleted.
- **A name found to be the library's:** a name row's `library_artist_id` is the library's artist when exactly one artist a name can be has its key (`library_artists`): one the Artists list files, one a bound play's song credits, or one credited on a record a play was heard from, so every artist the lists give as the library's, with files or not, is found again by their key. An album name's `library_group_id` is that artist's record when exactly one of their records, with files or heard from, has a version whose title has its key (`record_named`). The lists count a name found so with the library's artist or record, so a record bought later takes its bound plays by their binding and a bonus track the edition lacks by its name, and the album stays one entry. Two of the library's artists with one key find no name.
- **When the catalog changes:** a resolve, a display rebuild and a metadata edit or its withdrawal call `catalog_changed`, which derives again the plays whose binding moved, links again a bound play whose credit or record is not what it is linked to, and finds every name again, all in the same write and without touching a play otherwise. A scan's write that retires a file finds the names again too, since the resolve that follows may be cut off. Linking plays finds again the names it touched, and every name when a row of the library's artist or record was made or went.
- **Merges:** the library's rows hold the artist as canonical when linked, and the list reads each one's canonical artist and adds together the rows that are one.
- **Lists:** ordered by counted plays, first local date or last, then by key and a row's number, so that a page never repeats or skips a tie. A library album lists under the first artist of its credit, whose key and the album's find it again.
- **Pages by key:** `heard_artist_history` and `heard_album_history` answer a key with the library's artist or record when the rules above find exactly one, so a key kept from before the record was bought still arrives, and otherwise with the name's plays. The library's pages still count only the plays bound to their songs.

## Last.fm

Last.fm integration is compiled behind the server's default-on `lastfm` feature (`crates/ritornel-lastfm`, `apps/server/src/lastfm.rs`, `crates/ritornel-engine/src/{import_store,delivery_store}.rs`). API credentials come from `SCROBL_API_KEY` (`KEY_VARIABLE`) and `SCROBL_API_SECRET` (`SECRET_VARIABLE`), or `lastfm.env` (`CREDENTIALS_FILE`, owner-readable only) in the server data directory; the signed-in account's `session_key` lives only in `server.db`'s `service_session` and is never logged or sent to clients.

- **Import (`lastfm.sync` job):** Runs on the hub on manual request (`lastfm.sync`), on server startup, when a non-CLI/non-agent client connects more than `IMPORT_ON_OPEN_AFTER` (1 hour) after the last import, and every `IMPORT_EVERY` (6 hours). `LastfmSource` reads spans of up to 5,000 scrobbles (200 per page), re-reading the trailing 14 days (`recent_s`) on incremental syncs to catch late arrivals. Plays are stored as `observation_import` rows referencing `scrobble_subject` source items, and loved tracks are reconciled in `listener_rating`.
- **Duplicates from earlier imports:** Before the same-second paging fix, a read whose pages shifted between requests could list one scrobble of a second twice and another not at all, and store the first twice under two ordinals. A full sync (`lastfm.sync` with `full`, `ritornelctl lastfm sync --full`) sets such duplicates aside, in each batch's transaction (`recheck` in `import_store.rs`). It takes only that shape: a second whose first read stored as many imports as Last.fm lists now, but more of one track and fewer of another. Of the track stored more often, the imports Last.fm does not list get an `exclude` correction each on the manual endpoint, with a note saying why (`DUPLICATE_NOTE`) and `shifted_read` as the `reason` in its payload, never so many that fewer of the track count than Last.fm lists. A scrobble deleted at Last.fm, or added to it later, changes how many the second holds and is never taken for a duplicate. The import stays and its play is marked excluded; the server logs how many each batch set aside, and the corrections, with their note, are the record of it, in the library and in an export. An import whose play holds a listen is never set aside, so an echo that joined a listen's play never excludes that play. One a Spotify stream joined may be: a scrobble set aside is offered to no stream, so the stream keeps its play; an import set aside once is not set aside again, even after its correction is revoked; and when Last.fm lists a set-aside import's key again, a later full sync revokes its correction if it still stands. An incremental sync sets nothing aside. No call of the contract revokes a correction yet.
- **Scrobbling and Now Playing:** Eligible plays (`ELIGIBILITY_VERSION = 1`: counted, dated, trusted clock, `observed`, not excluded, not already present on that account, within `SEND_WITHIN_MS = 13 days`, at or after `scrobble_from_ms`) are tracked durably in `history_delivery`. A delivery moves from `pending` to `sending` in its own committed transaction *before* the HTTP request leaves, then settles to `accepted` (storing service-corrected names), `ignored` (storing the service code), `refused`, `unknown` (unanswered; never resent), or `withdrawn` (no longer eligible before sending). A never-attempted pending delivery (`attempts == 0`) whose play's metadata changed is refreshed in place before sending instead of withdrawn; once an attempt has been made (`attempts > 0`), changed track names (`artist`, `title`, `album`, `album_artist`, `track_number`) withdraw the delivery so a play whose prior attempt may have reached the service is not re-sent under different track metadata. A play is withdrawn with reason `ALREADY_SCROBBLED` when the account already has an active, non-excluded scrobble of the same track from another player within 1 second.
- **Restore reconciliation holds:** A backup of a library with any `service_account` writes per-account holds in `library_setting` (`scrobbling.held_until_import.<account>`) alongside the legacy global key (`scrobbling.held_until_import`, kept for downgrade/revert safety) on the backup copy. Starting a hub on that restored library expands and removes the global key, stamps each hold, drains due local staged/outbox listens into the library before background workers start, and holds scrobbling and Now Playing for each account independently until that account finishes an import (`release_hold`). Releasing an account's hold runs a deterministic 1-to-1 maximum matching across its pending deliveries and unplanned listens within `SEND_WITHIN_MS` of the backup against active, non-excluded returning imports (exact-second for `hub_age` plays and pending rows; bounded by `time_precision_ms` for `server_wall` listens re-dated after a reboot), marking matches `accepted` so echoes join their plays without re-sending, and clears only that account's hold. Releasing also keeps a bounded per-account post-restore window (`scrobbling.restored.<account>`, pruned after `SEND_WITHIN_MS`) so satellite listens or deferred local outbox listens arriving after the first sync still reconcile during `ingest_listen` before planning. Legacy global `scrobbling.held_until_import` holds from older backups are expanded to per-account holds on open or release.
- **Batching and backoff:** Sent in batches up to `MAX_BATCH` (50). On Last.fm's daily limit (`DAILY_LIMIT`), affected deliveries defer by 1 hour (`DAILY_LIMIT_RETRY_MS`) and the account pauses for 1 hour (`DAILY_LIMIT_WAIT`). Refused or unanswered batches pause the account for 5 minutes (`REFUSED_WAIT`, `UNANSWERED_WAIT`) so held-back plays retry in order once the pause lifts. A rejected session key sets `service_session.sign_in_again = 1` and halts scrobbling until re-authenticated.
- **Record health:** Unanswered (`unknown`), `withdrawn`, and `refused` deliveries, `sign_in_again`, and failed imports surface in `server.status` (`docs/internals/server.md`, "Record health").

## Spotify streams

Spotify's extended streaming history is a second source of import records beside Last.fm. A **stream** is one row of it. `ritornelctl spotify read <archive>` (`apps/ritornelctl/src/spotify/`) reads it on the machine it sits on, from the zip file Spotify sent or the folder it was unpacked to, without unpacking or copying it, and stores and sends nothing. `ritornelctl spotify import <archive>` reads it the same way, sends the kept records to the hub as a staged run, and shows the dry run of that run. Nothing is written to the library.

### Reading the archive

- **Files:** `archive.rs` reads a zip file in place, or a folder it was unpacked to, following links and passing over one that leads nowhere unless it is named as a history file, and reads files named `Streaming_History_Audio_*.json` (or `endsong_*.json` in older archives) and `Streaming_History_Video_*.json`, in name order. A music video's row in the video history is read exactly as a song's: the same fields, rules and play rule, with no mark of its own; its video shows are episodes, left out as audio episodes are. An archive with only `StreamingHistory*.json` is the account data's history, a year at minute precision with no track IDs, and is refused with the name of the export to ask for.
- **Kept fields:** `rows.rs` builds each row into a `SpotifyStream` (`ritornel-contract`): `ts` as `endedAt`, `ms_played`, the track URI, title, artist and album, `reason_start`, `reason_end`, `shuffle`, `skipped`, `offline`, `offline_timestamp` (kept as given, since its unit is undocumented), `incognito_mode`, and the platform reduced to three words for the device that made the sound, never one that controlled it, each left out when the text does not say (`device_words`): its `Platform`, the operating system only (`ios`, `android`, `mac`, `windows`, `linux`, `other`); its `DeviceKind`, the hardware the sound came out of (`computer`, `phone`, `tablet`, `watch`, `speaker` for a speaker, a smart display or an AV receiver, `tv`, `car`, `console`, `group` for speakers played together, `other`); and its `PlayedVia`, how that device played it (`app`, `web`, `cast` for Google Cast, `connect` for a Spotify Connect receiver with no app of its own, `airplay`, `other`). An app on a phone, a tablet or a computer is `app`, and a phone is not guessed from Android or a bare `ios`; a web player is `web`, with its system and `computer` when it names one; a bare `cast` is `cast` alone; a Spotify Connect `Partner` is read by its maker and model: a television, a set-top box or a console plays in an app of its own and has the platform `other`, Google's speakers, displays and dongles are cast to, its groups are a `group`, and a receiver or a network speaker is `connect`. `other` is named but outside the words, and Spotify's `unknown` is no word. The words are `ritornel_contract`'s (`device.rs`), for any source to use: one a reader does not know is read as `other`, never refused, so a newer side never breaks an older one. `SpotifyStream` refuses any field it does not name.
- **Dropped fields:** `ip_addr`, `ip_addr_decrypted`, `user_agent_decrypted`, `username` and `conn_country` are skipped as each row is parsed, so their values are never read into anything. Any field the reader does not know is skipped the same way and remembered by name only. The platform's text is read only to choose its words, from how it begins, and is in no record and no output.
- **Rows that are not music:** a row whose episode or audiobook fields hold anything (`EPISODE`, `AUDIOBOOK`, by exact name), a URI that is not `spotify:track:`, or no artist or title is counted by kind (`NotMusic`) and not kept.
- **Repeats:** rows with the same end time, time played and track URI are one stream reported more than once. The reader keeps every row and counts the repeats, and those that differ from the first in another kept field.
- **Errors:** each kept value is read on its own, so a row with a kept field of the wrong type, or a number too large for any type, is counted and named by file, row number and field name, and the file reads on. A file that is not a JSON list of rows, or a history file the zip cannot give, fails the read with its own name and the row it broke at. No error passes on a parser's message, which can quote the input, and a name is printed without the folders before it and with control characters made `?`.

### Matching streams to scrobbles

`ritornel_domain::streams` decides which stream and which Last.fm scrobble are one listen. It is pure and asks nothing of the library, so owning a record never changes which reports are one listen.

- **Events:** streams with the same end time, time played and track URI are one event, reported more than once. They are ordered by their other kept fields (`repeat_order`), with their names only to break a tie, so the order rows arrive in never decides which speaks; the first speaks for the event.
- **Names:** `name_key` compares artist and title after Unicode NFKC and case folding, with white space and punctuation, brackets of every kind among it, removed. Accents, symbols, credits and version words stay. The album is not compared, and in tier C a name that folds to nothing agrees with nothing.
- **Tiers:** each runs over what the last left, and within a tier the closest pair in time is taken first, ties going to the lower keys, so the joins never depend on the order records arrive in.

| Tier | A scrobble joins an event when |
|---|---|
| A, `Tier::Start` | It names the same track and is stamped within `WITHIN_MS` (10 s) of the event's worked-out start, its end less the time played. One scrobble to an event |
| B, `Tier::During` | It names the same track and is stamped while the event played, up to 10 s past its end. An event with no scrobble takes it as its own when it played more than 15 s (`NEVER_COUNTS_MS`), so a skip takes no scrobble from the play after it; then an event that has one takes it as a second report, stamped from as early as tier A takes one |
| C, `Tier::Renamed` | It is stamped as in A, the artist or the title agrees, each is the other's only candidate, the event played 30 s or more, and neither has a record of its own names within `PARTNER_WITHIN_MS` (an hour) |

- **In force:** every tier (`Tiers::ALL`), in derivation and in the dry run alike. `Tiers` with fewer is for weighing one tier against another in tests.

- **Offered scrobbles:** a listener's streams are offered that listener's scrobbles alone. A scrobble an `exclude` correction that stands sets aside, as one an earlier read stored twice is, is offered to no stream, by the corrections as derivation reads them and never by what a play of it shows, and neither is the echo of a listen's delivery (`echo_of`), a second copy of one included: a listen and a stream are two players.
- **Pins:** `Pins` names pairs a `split` or `join` correction that stands has kept apart or joined, read before the tiers: a stream by its key and a scrobble by its observation, so the same pins hold in any list of streams. `streams::pins` reads them from the corrections, for derivation and the dry run alike: of two that pin one listen, by any of its rows, and one scrobble, the newer says it. No correction writes them yet.
- **A stream alone:** `judge_alone` counts an event with no scrobble when it played four minutes, never when it played 15 s or less, and otherwise when it played half of its track's length. `lengths` takes a track's length as the median time played among its events that ended `trackdone` and played over 30 s, the lower of two middles. With no length it does not count and its outcome is `unknown`. Not counted, it is `skipped` when `reason_end` says the listener moved on (`moved_on`) and `partial` otherwise.

### Dry run

`imports.stage` sends a run in pieces, each of at most `MAX_STREAMS_PER_PIECE` (1,000) streams and well under a message's limit, and `imports.dryRun` starts a job (`imports.dryRun`, `apps/server/src/imports.rs`) that works out what applying the run would do. `imports.get` returns the run and its last report (`ImportReport`).

- **Staging:** the hub keeps a run in `<data-dir>/imports/<run>/` (`Staging`): `run.json`, one `piece-<n>.json` for each piece, as `SpotifyStream` values, `report.json`, the last dry run's, and `applied.json`, the apply's, which is all an applied run shows. Every file is owner-only and written whole or not at all. A run is removed a day after it was begun (`KEEP_MS`), looked for every hour, and at every start. The same piece sent again with the same streams is kept once, and said in `run.json` if it was not; with other streams it is a `conflict`. A piece with a stream that cannot be one (`SpotifyStream::plausible`: a `spotify:track:` URI, an artist and a title, an end from 1970 to 2100, at most a day played) is refused, as is staging on a server with no library. A report is kept only for the run it read: one that expired or was staged again meanwhile fails the job. A run applied is not dry run again, and keeps the report it was applied with.
- **Keys:** `stream_report::keyed` gives each stream its key from the records and the streams stored already, never from a clock of ours: `sp1`, its end, its time played, its track's ID, and its place among the streams that share those three, ordered by their other kept fields. Within such a group a stream takes the place of a stored stream with the same record; then one that differs from a stored stream left over in its names alone takes its place, as a row the archive spells otherwise; and the rest take places no stored stream has. So the same archive gives the same keys, and a later one gives the rows it shares with an earlier one their keys wherever its new rows sort.
- **Report:** `stream_report::dry_run` runs the matcher over the listener's scrobbles, read as of one commit (`LibraryStore::stream_dry_run`) with the corrections' pins, for what applying would do, with a sample of what tiers B and C join for judging them. It takes a track's length from every listener's streams in force, as derivation does, and counts what a length the run gives moves of another listener's plays. A stream in force for another listener, or stored for them and brought back into force by the run, stays theirs. It reads every stream in force once the run is applied, the run's and those in force now, and takes from the plays now what the streams in force add to them, so an archive in force already promises no play it does not add. By year of each event's worked-out start in UTC it counts listens, joins by tier and those a correction pins, events alone and counted, not counted or unknown, scrobbles no stream took, and plays and counted plays now and once applied; a second report joins the play it repeats, so it takes one from the plays after. It counts offline streams and whether each offline time reads as seconds or milliseconds near the stream's end, and the scrobbles no stream took that sit where a pause would put them: after the stream before a stream alone of their names and before its worked-out start, each counted once. `ImportProblem` lists what stands in the way: streams no zone covers.


### Applying and undoing

`imports.apply` and `imports.undo` are jobs (`APPLY_JOB`, `UNDO_JOB` in `apps/server/src/imports.rs`), each one library write whose command is the job's step (`step_command`), in which Spotify's tracks are judged against the library too ("Scrobble matching" in `docs/internals/identity.md`). An apply taken up again after a crash finds its run applied before it looks for the staged run, which the restart removed, and ends as done. A command sent again is answered with the job it started before anything it needed is looked at again. `ritornelctl spotify apply <run>`, `spotify undo <run>` and `spotify runs` drive them; `imports.list` lists the runs.

- **Apply** (`LibraryStore::apply_streams`) of a run not applied yet is refused while the run has no report, or the grouper, which the stream matcher is part of, or the play rule has changed since its dry run (`stale`); a run applied already needs neither, and its job ends as done. It works the report out again in the same write, from the library as that write sees it, and that report is the run's, kept in `applied.json`; if it cannot be kept, the run shows none. In one write it makes the `import` endpoint and the `spotify` source instance named `Spotify` if they are missing, stores each stream whose key the endpoint does not hold, records the run (`import_run`) with the reader, a SHA-256 of the archive's kept records in their own order, the same for the same archive whatever is stored, its counts and the session that asked, every stream its archive held (`import_run_stream`), new or not, and an `applied` row of its ledger (`import_run_event`). A run applied before writes nothing.
- **A stream as stored:** a `history_observation` of kind `import` keyed by its source key, with `payload_json` written from the typed `SpotifyStream` as it is kept (`kept`: without what it played on), never from text it was handed; an `observation_import` row holding the worked-out start in `played_at_utc_ms` and the names as that archive spelled them; an `observation_import_stream` row with the end as reported, time played, the reasons, the flags and the offline time as given; and a `spotify_track` source item for its URI, bound to the library by the scrobble matcher on the names of its newest stream in force (`match_library`, "Scrobble matching" in `docs/internals/identity.md`). A stored stream is read back from these typed columns (`stored_stream`), never from its `payload_json`, which is its archive. It keeps the record it was stored with: a row of a later archive with the same end, time played and track takes its place, first one the same, then one spelled otherwise in its names alone, then any, and is counted in the dry run (`respelled`) when it differs, and changes nothing.
- **What it played on** is kept apart, in `stream_device`, since a later reader may say it better: its `platform`, `device_kind` and `via` words, each NULL when the text did not say, and the run whose reader said them. Every run applied says them again for each stream it holds, stored before or not, names itself as the run that said them, and stores no new row for it, so no play changes. Nothing derived reads them. A word added later is this table made again with its checks, and the contract's word: no stream's row is touched. An export gives them in each stream's `stream` object as `platform`, `deviceKind` and `playedVia`.
- **Undo** (`LibraryStore::undo_streams`) adds an `undone` row to the run's ledger. A stream is in force while any run that held it is, so undoing one run leaves in force what another holds. Nothing is deleted, and importing the archive again is a new run that brings its streams back.
- **Every play is derived again** in the same write (`derive_every_play`): a stream in force or out of it can move its track's length, and so any play of it. Undoing every run leaves every play, ID and count as it was before the first was applied.

### Reading streams

Derivation reads the streams in force (`history_streams.rs`) beside the scrobbles, which leave streams out (`NOT_A_STREAM` in `history_store.rs`), and each track's length from its streams in force.

| Of a play a stream speaks for | Comes from |
|---|---|
| Time | Its end less the time played, `time_anchor = 'end'`, `clock_basis = 'source_utc'`, to the second, in the listener's zone then |
| Time played, shuffle | The stream |
| Binding | The stream's `spotify_track` item, or a `set_recording` correction on any of its observations |
| Whether it counts | With a scrobble joined as its own, the scrobble's verdict (`count_basis = 'source_verdict'`, `listened`); alone, `judge_alone` (`count_basis = 'play_rule'`) |

- **Offered scrobbles:** a scrobble set aside, or the echo of a listen's delivery, is offered to no stream.
- **Incremental derivation:** the records near an import, which an incremental derive widens to (`neighbours` in `history_store.rs`, called from `rederive`), take in every stream and scrobble a tier could join it with, those of its own names within the hour that decides tier C, and a stream's repeats (`history_streams::neighbours`), transitively. For a scrobble that is every import within the hour, and any longer stream still playing when it was stamped. The matcher pairs closest first within what it is given, so a closed set gives the joins a rebuild gives: a scrobble that a sync brings after its stream joins it as a rebuild would join it.
- **Never scrobbled:** a play a stream speaks for has no listen as its primary, so it is never eligible to be sent.
- **The Last.fm report** counts the account's scrobbles joined to a stream (`joinedToStreams`) apart from its echoes, and leaves both out of its plays.

## Export

`history.export` (`crates/ritornel-engine/src/export.rs`, `apps/server/src/dispatch/export.rs`, `ritornelctl export`) writes a portable, self-contained directory (`ritornel-record-<timestamp>`, staged under a dotted name and atomically renamed, with owner-only file permissions) from a single read-only SQLite transaction (`EXPORT_FORMAT = "ritornel-record"`, `EXPORT_VERSION = 3`):

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `manifest.json` | Format, version, library ID, export timestamp, rule/grouper versions, endpoints, listeners, sources, and line counts. |
| `observations.jsonl` | Every observation oldest first, keyed by endpoint and `source_key`, with verbatim payloads. A stream's import line carries a `stream` object with what only a stream knows, and the words for what it played on: `platform`, `deviceKind`, `playedVia`. |
| `ratings.jsonl` | Every rating and love row, including clears. |
| `deliveries.jsonl` | Every scrobble delivery and its outcome, enabling exact echo matching outside Ritornel. |
| `runs.jsonl` | Every run of streams applied, with its reader, records hash, counts and ledger, so a reader outside Ritornel can tell which streams are in force. |
| `run_streams.jsonl` | The streams each run's archive held, by run, endpoint and `source_key`. |
| `plays.csv` | Derived plays table with current track display names for immediate spreadsheet/script use. |

## Decisions

- **Immutable observations plus pure versioned derivation.** Keeping raw listens, imports, and corrections immutable while versioning `PlayRule` and `GROUPER_VERSION` means rule changes or bug fixes are safe full rebuilds.
- **Monotonic boot-age dating and bundled IANA TZDB.** Dating listens by monotonic age within a boot neutralizes skewed local wall clocks, and bundling the time-zone database makes local dates reproducible across machines.
- **No rollup tables.** Indexed scans over `play_event` are fast enough for large histories and eliminate cache-invalidation drift between raw plays and displayed counts. The heard links are not a rollup: one row a play, written and dropped with it, so they cannot drift from the plays; they exist so that no list folds every play's names as it is read.
- **Commit `sending` before HTTP scrobble send.** Persisting `history_delivery` before calling Last.fm guarantees exact echo matching on subsequent imports and prevents duplicate resends after network timeouts.
- **Self-contained plain-text export.** Exporting observations, ratings, deliveries, and derived plays as JSON Lines, JSON, and CSV ensures the entire listening record remains readable and re-derivable without Ritornel.
- **Two reports are one listen by their times and names alone.** The stream matcher never asks the library, so buying a record cannot change which old reports are one listen. All three tiers are in force: B and C were judged on a sample of what each joins on real records first, and where C's names disagree the stream's track speaks for the play.
- **A run is staged and dry run before anything is written.** Only the kept fields cross to the hub, in pieces under the socket's limit, and the dry run is a job because a call through a satellite gives up after eight seconds.
- **Spotify's fields are taken by name.** The reader names the fields it keeps, not the ones it drops, so a field Spotify adds later is dropped without a decision. Values of dropped fields never leave the parser, and the reader prints counts and field names, never a value.
